# Playlist Playback Pitfalls Research

**Milestone:** v1.0 Playlist Playback & Management  
**Researched:** 2026-10-10  
**Confidence:** MEDIUM

## Critical Pitfalls

### Store resolved URLs instead of Home Assistant identifiers

URLs can expire, encode access details, or change when providers refresh their catalog. Store media-source IDs and resolve each item when it is about to play. Never log or persist resolved URLs or credentials.

### Give the ingress UI an add-on-side HA token

The ingress client currently calls only the add-on API; it has no HA connection. Copying an HA token into the frontend or add-on would widen the credential boundary. Add an HA-owned authenticated catalog operation and test that tokens never reach browser storage, add-on settings, or logs.

### Advance a queue on every process exit

FFmpeg exits can mean natural completion, explicit stop, replacement, or failure. Only natural successful completion should advance. Stop/replacement/disconnect must invalidate stale callbacks, and failures need a visible bounded reason.

### Assume every provider playlist is one URL

Providers may expose a playlist as a collection, a playlist resource, or a stream URL. Browse and resolve through HA APIs; verify representative provider shapes and fail clearly for unsupported items.

### Treat selection of multiple speakers as an implicit synchronized queue

Existing per-speaker process ownership does not guarantee synchronized item advancement. Keep this milestone's playlist behavior per selected speaker unless synchronized queues are explicitly scoped and designed.

## Looks-Done-But-Isn't Checks

- [ ] A provider playlist browses successfully and resolves to media the add-on can reach.
- [ ] A saved list survives HA/add-on restart and stale provider IDs fail visibly.
- [ ] Natural item completion advances exactly once; stop, replacement, failure, and disconnect do not.
- [ ] A closed ingress tab does not halt playback or strand queue state.
- [ ] HA tokens and resolved URLs are absent from persistence, diagnostics, and logs.
